What I value

What matters is not only what I build, but who I build it for and how I approach it. These are the principles that guide my decisions in software development and learning.

Software designed around people.

Rather than maximizing features, I value software that people can use without getting lost or forcing themselves into someone else's workflow. I aim for interfaces where the flow of actions and placement of information feel natural.

My motto: “Challenge” and “Contribution”

Challenge

Start by trying what I don't know.

Even when something is unfamiliar, I start by touching it and trying it. Failed attempts and detours are part of the process, and I turn them into the next lesson.

Turn “what if” into something that works.

I don't stop at using what already exists. When I think, “What if it worked like this?”, I build my own version and take even small ideas as far as a working result.

Contribution

Turn my solution into someone else's shortcut.

Rather than using technology only for myself, I want to turn what I learn into something that reduces someone else's frustration or helps the next person move forward.

Pass what I receive on to the next person.

I try not to keep what I learn to myself. I share it with teammates and younger students, and publish problem-solving processes on GitHub so the next person can start with a better path.
